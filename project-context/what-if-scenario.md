					                      			Theft
T1: A passenger steals something from the driver’s car
Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
We verify both drivers and riders with ID and a selfie match before their first trip. Before each ride, both sides see the other’s verified profile, vehicle plate and route. Ratings build trust over time, and new accounts have limits. Drivers are reminded to lock valuables away. Confirmed theft leads to a permanent ban and a police report, so being traceable discourages it.
During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
The app cannot stop a theft, but it gets help moving faster. Trips can be shared live with a trusted contact. A hands-free SOS can be triggered by voice command, pressing the power button several times, or shaking the phone, so it works even if someone cannot use the screen. It alerts the trusted contact and offers a one-tap 911 call. If something looks unusual, like an unexpected drop-off or a long stop, the app checks in with both people. Trip time, route and plate are logged as evidence.
After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
The victim reports the incident in the app with the trip ID. We freeze the reported account and preserve the trip data. Police lead any investigation, and we share data on valid legal request. Compensation goes through the owner’s insurance, since the platform is a connector, not an insurer. The accused can respond and appeal, and our support team follows up with both parties.
T2: A driver leaves with the passenger’s belongings
Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
Drivers are verified with ID and a selfie match, and riders see the driver’s verified profile, plate and rating before the ride. The app reminds riders to check their belongings before leaving the car, and reminds drivers to check the back seat. Confirmed theft leads to a permanent ban and a police report.
During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
At drop-off, both sides confirm in the app that the ride ended, with a “forgot something?” prompt for the rider. If the rider realises later, they can message the driver through the app using masked phone numbers, with no need to share personal contact details. A hands-free SOS (voice, power button or shake) is available if the rider feels unsafe while trying to get the item back. We tell riders to call 911 rather than confront the driver.
After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
The rider reports the lost item with the trip ID. The app contacts the driver to arrange a return at a safe public spot, and if the driver does not respond or refuses, the account is frozen and trip data is preserved. Police lead any investigation, and we share data on valid legal request. Compensation goes through the rider’s own insurance, since the platform is a connector, not an insurer. The driver can respond and appeal.
T3: Someone reports a theft and the two people give different accounts
Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
Every trip leaves a record: time, route, plate and both users’ verified identities. Both sides agree to a clear dispute policy at sign-up. Optionally, a rider and driver can confirm at pickup and drop-off what is in the car, such as large bags. A report requires specific details (item, time, location), which reduces false claims.
During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
When a report is filed, the app immediately preserves the trip data and notifies the other person, who can respond with their side. Both people can add details and evidence, such as photos or messages sent through the app. Neither person is penalised while the case is open. A hands-free SOS stays available throughout.
After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
A trained support team reviews both accounts alongside the trip data and decides, using the evidence rather than who reported first. If it is unclear, we do not punish either side, but we note the case, and repeated patterns lead to a review or ban. Both users can appeal. Anything that looks like a crime goes to police, who decide guilt, not the app. False reports are treated as a policy violation.

TR2: What if picking someone up adds much more time than the driver agreed to?
Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
Before each trip, the driver sets a hard limit: a maximum detour (e.g. 5 minutes) and a latest arrival time. The app only matches riders who fit inside that limit, using live traffic estimates, and shows the driver the exact added time before they accept. Riders see a clear pickup window and a short wait limit. Drivers can also mark days when they cannot be flexible at all.
During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
The app recalculates the route in real time. If a pickup would push the driver past their limit, the app skips it automatically, with no action needed from the driver. Alerts are voice-based, so a driver does not need to look at the screen while driving. If the rider is late, the wait timer ends after a short time and the driver can leave with one tap, or by voice command. The affected rider is notified immediately and offered a rematch or an alternative ride.
After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
A skipped or abandoned rider is refunded automatically and prioritized for the next match. A rider who did not show up pays a small no-show fee that goes to the driver. If a trip still ran over the driver’s limit, the driver can report it, and the app reviews the trip data, then adjusts matching for that route and compensates the driver if the app was at fault. Repeated problems affect ratings and matching priority for both sides. Our support team handles disputes.

TR3: What if a road closure forces the driver away from the agreed route?
Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
Before each trip, the app checks live traffic and road closure data on the planned route and warns the driver and riders if a closure is likely. It also stores a backup route. The match only includes riders who would still be reasonably served if the driver takes the backup route. Both sides see a clear policy upfront: the driver’s safe arrival comes first, and the route may change if the road is closed.
During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
If a closure is detected, the app reroutes the driver automatically and gives voice directions, so the driver does not need to look at the screen. It recalculates which riders can still be picked up or dropped off within the driver’s detour limit. Riders are notified right away with the new route and updated arrival time. If a rider’s stop is no longer reachable, the app proposes the nearest safe alternative stop, or the driver can skip it with one tap or a voice command. All riders can share the live trip with a trusted contact, so they are not left stranded without anyone knowing.
After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
A rider who was dropped off early, skipped or delayed because of a closure is refunded in full or partly, and is prioritized for the next match. The driver is not penalized, since closures are outside their control. Trip data, including the closure and the reroute, is logged, so disputes can be checked against it. Our support team handles complaints. If the same road closes often, the app learns to avoid that route in future matches.

TR4: What if the driver speeds or drives dangerously to avoid arriving late?
1. Before: How could we reduce the chance of it happening?
We remove the pressure to speed. Drivers set their own detour limit and arrival buffer, and the app only matches riders who fit with a safety margin. ETAs use realistic traffic estimates, not best-case times. Drivers agree to a safe-driving policy at sign-up and are screened (valid licence, driving record check where available). The app never rewards speed: no bonuses for fast pickups, and no penalties for a driver who declines a pickup to stay on time.
2. During: What can the app actually do, including when someone cannot use their phone?
With the driver’s consent, the phone’s sensors detect speeding, harsh braking and sharp turns. The driver gets a calm voice warning, with no need to look at the screen. If the behaviour continues, the app can ask riders privately, “Do you feel safe?”, which they can answer with one tap. Riders can trigger a hands-free SOS (voice command, power button or shake) that alerts a trusted contact and offers a 911 call. Riders can also share their trip live and ask to be dropped off at the next safe spot.
3. After: What happens afterward, and who handles the follow-up?
Rider reports and sensor data are reviewed together by our support team. Repeated or serious events lead to a warning, suspension or permanent ban, and the rider is refunded. Serious danger or an accident goes to police, and we share trip data on valid legal request. Drivers can see their own safe-driving history and appeal a decision if the data was wrong. Insurance handles any damage or injury claims.


															Privacy
P1: Someone learns your home address before you agree to ride together
Before:
Exact addresses are never shown before both sides accept a match. Until then, users only see an approximate area and a time window. Users can choose a public pickup spot (e.g. a nearby corner or transit stop) instead of their door.
During:
After a match is confirmed, the exact pickup point is shared only with the matched driver or rider, only for that trip. Riders in the same car never see each other’s addresses. Calls and messages go through the app with masked phone numbers.
After:
The address disappears from the other person’s view once the trip ends. Users can delete saved addresses at any time, and report anyone who misuses an address, which leads to suspension.
P2: Someone uses your recurring schedule to learn when you’re away from home
Before:
Recurring rides are optional, and users see only one trip at a time, never the full weekly pattern. Other users see time windows, not exact departure times, and no one can browse another person’s schedule.
During:
Users have a one-tap “pause all trips” button for holidays or time away. The app flags unusual behaviour, such as one user repeatedly viewing the same person’s trips, and warns the person being viewed.
After:
Past trip history is visible only to the user, not to others, and is deleted after a short period. Users can see who viewed their trips and block or report them.
P3: You block someone but they can still see your future trips
Before:
Blocking is total and works in both directions. It applies to profiles, search, matching and all current and future trips, and is enforced on our server, not just in the app.
During:
The moment you block someone, shared upcoming trips with them are cancelled and their access is removed immediately, including any live-share links. The blocked person is not told they were blocked, only that no match is available, to reduce the risk of retaliation.
After:
We run regular checks that blocked users cannot see blocked users’ data, and any leak is treated as a top-priority bug. Users can report a blocked person who still gets through, and support follows up and can ban the account.
P4: Your location stays visible after the ride ends
Before:
Location sharing is tied only to an active trip. It starts when the trip starts and ends automatically when it ends, and the server enforces this even if the app is closed or crashes.
During:
A visible banner always shows who is seeing your location, with a one-tap “stop sharing”. Live-share links given to trusted contacts expire when the trip ends.
After:
Live location is deleted shortly after the trip, and the “who viewed me” log lets you check. If you ever see sharing still on, a “stop all sharing” button ends everything at once, and you can report it to support.
