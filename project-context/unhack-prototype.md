This guide records the Disco submission requirements and maps [Routemate’s agreed approach](approach.md) to the judges’ scorecard. The demo and pilot ideas below are proposed scope. Use the agreed approach for the product decisions.

## What to demonstrate

Start with one York Keele student who has a recurring class or lab and a difficult transit connection. Keele is the first campus for Routemate’s university carpooling prototype.

1. **Enter a recurring trip.** Show the rider’s pickup, destination, required arrival time and days. Show an existing driver route with its own required arrival time, recurring days, open seats and driver-chosen detour limit.
2. **Explain the match.** Highlight the shared days, available seat and estimated added travel time for pickup and drop-off. Show that both people can arrive by their deadlines. Show how ride preferences narrow the eligible matches, then demonstrate a case with no eligible ride.
3. **Confirm the routine.** Show the same driver, the recurring pickup days and time, and the agreed contribution per ride. For the agreed backup and arrival check-in features, show a short follow-on state. Use an eligible backup only if one is available in the scenario, and show the unavailable state otherwise. Label any simulated screens or data and disclose any scope cuts.

Aim to make the core flow understandable in about a minute. The build lesson calls for a one-page team spec covering the user, screens and data, what must work, what can wait, and one owner per piece. Ask someone outside the team to try the core flow before submission. Use fictional or anonymized trips in a public demo.

**Use the guided demo.** The [frontend guide](../frontend/README.md) shows a preset Malton-to-Keele rider trip and a no-match screen opened with its preview button. The fuller screens show backup matching and arrival check-in. Use the prefilled demo details and describe the people, trips and acceptances as simulated. Any on-screen travel time or savings figure is an estimate.

**Sample submission description.** Routemate is an interactive web prototype for university students who want a reliable carpool to class, starting at York Keele. It uses a simulated trip to show how a rider finds a driver already making a compatible commute and sees the recurring pickup days, pickup time, and contribution per ride before confirming.

## How Routemate can earn points

Each of the seven criteria is scored from 1 to 4, for a total of 28. The scorecard prioritizes validation over build polish. It breaks ties first on the problem statement, then on the innovative solution and unique value proposition, and then on pitch clarity and Q&A.

| Criterion | What to show or substantiate |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem statement** | Use the team’s agreed How Might We statement and illustrate it with one actual trip. Describe the fixed start time, what happens when a connection fails, and how often it costs the rider time or money. Test whether the bigger issue is a shortage of affordable options, trust and coordination, or both. |
| **Target persona** | Make one Keele student concrete with a recurring trip, arrival deadline, pickup flexibility and current workaround. Show the compatible driver’s existing route and detour limit. Mark persona assumptions. |
| **Solution** | Walk through the recurring commitment and explain how the route, schedule, seat and detour checks protect both arrival deadlines. Show what happens when no driver fits. |
| **Unique value proposition** | Test whether a repeat commitment with the same driver and an agreed pickup and cost is valuable beyond the initial match. Ask people what they already use and what would make them switch from transit, informal lifts, ride-hail, York Smart Commute or Poparide. Treat differentiation as a hypothesis until tested. |
| **Validation and market feedback** | Speak with student riders and drivers already making compatible campus trips. Record the actual trip or decision they described, what you learned, and what you changed after feedback. Relevant student conversations, mentor or expert feedback, and quick surveys count under the scorecard. Label [Peter’s simulated interviews](peter.md) and distinguish sourced background numbers from firsthand evidence. |
| **Impact and SDG alignment** | Lead with **SDG 11** and access to reliable transportation. Compare pilot trips with the same riders’ usual commute using on-time arrivals, time budgeted as a buffer, and actual cost per trip. Give the sample size and timeframe. Describe SDG 9 as using existing transport capacity, SDG 16 as a proposed link through clear commitments and privacy, and SDG 17 as a potential campus or commuter-program partnership. Present projected benefits as hypotheses and only estimate emissions if you know what trips the carpool replaces and how much detour it adds. |
| **Technical feasibility and business model** | Pilot one corridor and one arrival window. Explain how to find enough overlapping seats, keep detours acceptable and track repeat-trip completion. Separate the rider’s contribution to the driver from potential Routemate revenue. Identify coordination, hosting and support costs. Treat campus partnership or funding and any service fee as untested options. |
