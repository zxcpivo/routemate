# Prompt 4: Design Your Value Proposition

Challenge 5: Connected, Safe & Future-Ready Communities

A Value Proposition Canvas prompt used to test whether our carpooling idea fits its customers.

## Prompt

```
Don't flatter me. If the evidence is thin, say so.

Act as a startup strategist using the Value Proposition Canvas. Help me test whether my solution actually fits my customer.

My customer (ICP): Daily commuters in the Greater Toronto Area (e.g. Scarborough, North York, Etobicoke) who travel to work during peak hours and currently rely on TTC/GO Transit, with Uber as an occasional backup. Secondary side of the marketplace: car owners who drive to work alone along the same routes.

My problem statement: Commuters who depend on TTC face delays, long travel times and safety concerns, especially at night or at quiet stops. Uber solves this but is too expensive to use every day. Meanwhile, many drivers commute alone along the same routes and pay the full cost of gas and parking.

My solution idea: A mobile app that matches a driver who is already commuting to work with 2-3 riders whose pickup points and destinations are along the driver's existing route. The driver does not change their route, so detours stay small. Riders pay a lower fare than Uber, and the driver offsets part of their commute cost. The app earns revenue through a service fee on each ride.

Evidence so far: None yet. No interviews, sign-ups or pre-orders. All claims about rider pain, driver willingness and price sensitivity are assumptions that still need testing.

Ask me clarifying questions first if anything is vague. Then:
1. Customer side: list their jobs-to-be-done, pains (ranked by severity) and gains (ranked by importance).
2. Solution side: map my products/services, pain relievers and gain creators against those pains and gains.
3. Show where the fit is strong, weak or missing, and call out any features that solve a problem the customer didn't mention.
4. Write my value proposition as: "We help [who] solve [what], so they can [outcome]." Give me 3 versions, simplest first. Each one should pass the KISS test: understood in one go.
5. Suggest the 3 cheapest experiments to test whether customers actually value this, and what result would count as proof.

Challenge me if I'm describing features instead of outcomes.
```

## Notes

- Evidence is intentionally "none yet". Update it after interviews and experiments.
- ICP is a working assumption and should be narrowed to one route or area.
