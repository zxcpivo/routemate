# Peter: carpooling project notes

_Last updated: 2026-11-07_

## Challenge
**Bucket:** Connected, Safe & Future-Ready Communities

**Our HMW:**
> How might we help make transportation safer, more affordable, and more available for fixed schedule commuters (work/school commuters)?

**Sharper alternative:**
> How might we help fixed-schedule work and school commuters without a car get safe, affordable rides by connecting them with trusted neighbours who already drive the same route at the same time every day?

**Slide version:** How might we turn same-route, same-time neighbours into a trusted daily carpool?

## Idea (working name: RideCircle)
A trusted carpool network for **fixed-schedule commuters**. Riders without a car are matched with verified neighbours, coworkers or classmates who already drive the same route at the same time.

- **Recurring matches:** the same people, the same route, every day.
- **Verification:** ID/address, plus community circles (workplace, school, building).
- **Rider preferences:** optionally choose who you ride with (gender, age). Treat as secondary until interviews show it matters.
- **Safety:** live trip sharing, an arrival check-in, and an alert if you go quiet.
- **Backup:** a backup match when the regular driver is away, so the carpool doesn't die.
- **Cost sharing:** a predictable, agreed fee per ride.

**Why it's different from Uber, BlaBlaCar or Poparide:** trust comes from community, and matching is recurring, not one-off.

## Problem statement
**Original:**
> We believe fixed-schedule work and school commuters without a car struggle with getting to work or school safely, affordably and on time when transit is slow, infrequent or doesn't match their hours. Today it is 1–2 extra hours a day, $25–50 Uber rides when transit fails, and waits at stops early or late, while neighbours drive the same route at the same time alone.

**Refined (after simulated debrief, see below):**
> We believe fixed-schedule commuters without a car on routes with weak transit struggle with getting a reliable, safe ride when transit doesn't match their hours and informal carpools fall apart. Today it is late or missed shifts, $20–250 a month on emergency Ubers, waiting alone at dark stops, and depending on family, while drivers on the same route would take one familiar regular passenger if they could find one.

**Root cause:** not a lack of cars, but a lack of **trust and coordination** between people with overlapping routes.

## ICP
- **Primary (riders):** commute on a fixed schedule (same route and times, 4–5 days a week), live 10–40 km away, no car or can't afford to drive and park, and transit is slow (60+ min), needs 2+ transfers, or doesn't run at their hours.
- **Secondary (drivers):** drive that same route alone at the same times and pay their own gas and parking.
- **Not customers (so far):** rotating-shift workers and people with good transit.

## Riskiest assumptions
1. Unreliable or unsafe commutes are a frequent, real pain.
2. Trust is the blocker: people ride with a verified neighbour or coworker, not a stranger.
3. **Enough drivers** on the same routes will take a regular passenger. This is the biggest risk.

# Interview debrief

> ⚠️ **SIMULATED INTERVIEWS: hypotheses, not evidence.**
> The 6 interviews below were made up to show what we expect to hear. They are not real people. Replace them with real interviews before using any of this as evidence, or label it on slides as "assumptions based on simulated interviews."

## Interview notes (simulated)

**Interview 1:** 24, warehouse worker, rider, Brampton → Mississauga, 6am–2pm Mon–Fri, no car
- Commute: 2 buses, 75 min each way, ~$156/month transit
- Pain: "If I miss the 5:10 bus I'm late, and they've already written me up twice."
- Workarounds: Ubered 3x last month (~$28 each). Coworker drives him "only when he remembers."
- Time/money: ~12.5 hrs/week + $20–80/month on Uber
- Carpooled?: Yes, 2 months with a coworker. Ended when the coworker switched shifts.
- Stranger vs neighbour: "Not some random guy at 5am. A coworker, sure."
- Surprise: Being on time matters way more to him than cost.

**Interview 2:** 20, university student, rider, Vaughan → campus, classes Mon/Wed/Thu 10am–9pm, no car
- Commute: bus + subway, 65 min each way
- Pain: "Wednesday night I wait 35 minutes at the stop in the dark. My mom makes me text her the whole way."
- Workarounds: Mom picks her up on the worst nights; posts in a commuter Discord "anyone driving to Vaughan?"
- Time/money: ~8 hrs/week
- Carpooled?: Got rides from a classmate a few times. "Felt awkward asking every week."
- Stranger vs neighbour: "I'd only ride with a girl, or someone my friends know."
- Surprise: The problem isn't finding a ride, it's the awkwardness of asking repeatedly.

**Interview 3:** 38, nurse, rider, Markham → hospital, 7pm–7am rotating nights, no car (family has 1 car)
- Commute: 1 bus + walk, 50 min; no bus after midnight
- Pain: "My schedule changes every two weeks, so nothing regular works for me."
- Workarounds: Husband drives her, which means the kids get woken up; Uber home ~$35 when he can't.
- Time/money: ~$150–250/month on Uber
- Carpooled?: No. "Nobody has my exact shifts."
- Stranger vs neighbour: Wants a coworker from the same hospital.
- Surprise: A rotating schedule breaks the "fixed schedule" assumption.

**Interview 4:** 52, office admin, rider, Ajax → downtown, 9–5 Mon–Fri, no car (stopped driving)
- Commute: GO train + subway, 70 min, ~$300/month
- Pain: "Honestly it's fine. Long, but I read on the train."
- Workarounds: None needed.
- Carpooled?: No interest.
- Surprise: Good transit means no pain. She's not a customer.

**Interview 5:** 31, IT worker, DRIVER, Brampton → Mississauga, 8:30–5 Mon–Fri, drives alone
- Commute: 35 min drive, ~$60/week gas
- Pain: "Traffic sucks but it's my car, my music, my time."
- Would take riders?: "Maybe one regular person. Not a different stranger every day."
- Concerns: "What if they're late? What about insurance if we crash?"
- Has done it?: Drove a coworker for 6 months. "It was nice, split gas, then she moved."
- Surprise: Money isn't the motivation. Company and splitting costs with someone familiar is.

**Interview 6:** 27, retail manager, DRIVER, Scarborough → Pickering, shifts vary weekly
- Commute: 25 min drive
- Would take riders?: "No. I leave whenever I'm done, I don't want to wait for anyone."
- Surprise: Flexibility and control matter more than gas savings. Not a good supply-side driver.

## 1. Interview summaries

**Interview 1: warehouse worker**
- Who: 24, early shift (6 am–2 pm), Brampton → Mississauga, no car.
- Pain: "If I miss the 5:10 bus I'm late, and they've already written me up twice."
- How they cope: 2 buses (75 minutes), Uber about 3 times a month (~$28 each), occasional rides from a coworker.

**Interview 2: university student**
- Who: 20, evening classes until 9 pm, Vaughan → campus, no car.
- Pain: "Wednesday night I wait 35 minutes at the stop in the dark."
- How they cope: her mom picks her up on the worst nights, she posts in a commuter Discord, and she occasionally gets rides from a classmate.

**Interview 3: nurse**
- Who: 38, rotating night shifts, Markham → hospital, family shares one car.
- Pain: "My schedule changes every two weeks, so nothing regular works for me."
- How they cope: her husband drives her, or she takes an Uber home (~$35), spending $150–250 a month.

**Interview 4: office admin**
- Who: 52, 9–5, Ajax → downtown, stopped driving.
- Pain: "Honestly it's fine. Long, but I read on the train."
- How they cope: GO train and subway. No workaround needed.

**Interview 5: IT worker (driver)**
- Who: 31, 8:30–5, Brampton → Mississauga, drives alone.
- Pain: "Maybe one regular person. Not a different stranger every day."
- How they cope: drove a coworker for 6 months and split gas, until she moved.

**Interview 6: retail manager (driver)**
- Who: 27, shifts change weekly, Scarborough → Pickering, drives alone.
- Pain: "I leave whenever I'm done, I don't want to wait for anyone."
- How they cope: drives alone and has no interest in passengers.

## 2. Recurring pains (ranked by how often and how strongly)
1. **Only trusting people they know (4 of 6, strong):** "Not some random guy at 5am. A coworker, sure." (#1)
2. **Unreliable transit at early or late hours (3 of 6, strong):** "If I miss the 5:10 bus I'm late, and they've already written me up twice." (#1)
3. **Carpools break when something changes (3 of 6, medium):** "It was nice, split gas, then she moved." (#5)
4. **Waiting alone at night feels unsafe (2 of 6, strong):** "I wait 35 minutes at the stop in the dark." (#2)
5. **Asking for rides is awkward (2 of 6, medium):** "Felt awkward asking every week." (#2)

## 3. Real evidence vs. weak signals
**Real evidence (if the interviews were real):**
- $20–250 a month already spent on emergency Ubers (#1, #3).
- Carpools that happened and ended (#1: 2 months, #5: 6 months).
- Family members driving them as a backup (#2, #3).
- "Anyone driving to Vaughan?" posts in Discord (#2).
- Written up twice for being late (#1).

**Weak signals:**
- "Maybe one regular person" (#5) is a hypothetical.
- "I'd only ride with a girl" (#2) is a preference, not proof of use.
- "A coworker, sure" (#1) is a hypothetical.

## 4. Surprises
- **Money isn't the main pain.** Riders care about being on time and safe. Drivers care about control and company.
- **Carpools already happen and then die.** The gap is keeping them going, not starting them.
- **Rotating schedules break the "fixed schedule" ICP** (#3, #6).
- **Good transit means no customer** (#4).
- **Drivers want one familiar regular passenger,** not strangers.

## 5. Verdict: needs refining
> We believe fixed-schedule commuters without a car on routes with weak transit struggle with getting a reliable, safe ride when transit doesn't match their hours and informal carpools fall apart. Today it is late or missed shifts, $20–250 a month on emergency Ubers, waiting alone at dark stops, and depending on family, while drivers on the same route would take one familiar regular passenger if they could find one.

## 6. Next steps
**Interview next (for real):** early-shift warehouse or factory workers, students with evening classes, and more fixed-schedule drivers (the biggest risk).

**Add these questions:**
1. "Tell me about a carpool you had that ended. Why did it end?"
2. "What happened the last time you were late? What did it cost you?"
3. (Drivers) "What would make you stop driving someone?"

**Drop these questions:**
1. Gas savings and emissions (nobody raised them).
2. "Would you use…?" (it only produces weak signals).
3. Anything aimed at people with good transit (they aren't customers).
